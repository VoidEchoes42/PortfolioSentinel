# Methodology & Mathematical Assumptions

This document outlines the core quantitative methodologies and mathematical assumptions used in the PortfolioSentinel risk engines.

## 1. Market Risk Calculations

### Value at Risk (VaR)
Value at Risk estimates the potential loss in value of a portfolio over a defined period for a given confidence interval.
- **Parametric VaR**: Assumes portfolio returns are normally distributed.
  - Formula: `VaR = Portfolio Value * (Z-score * Portfolio Volatility)`
  - We calculate a 1-day 95% VaR (Z ≈ 1.645) and a 99% VaR (Z ≈ 2.33).
- **Historical VaR**: Non-parametric approach that looks at the actual historical distribution of daily returns and takes the 5th and 1st percentiles.

### Volatility
We utilize a **21-day rolling window** to calculate annualized volatility. 
- 21 days represents roughly one trading month. 
- Annualization factor: `sqrt(252)` (assuming 252 trading days in a year).

### Monte Carlo Simulation
Used for forward-looking risk assessment.
- **Process**: We simulate future price paths using Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM) assumptions.
- **Correlation**: To ensure the simulations respect the historical correlation between assets (e.g., tech stocks moving together), we compute the covariance matrix of historical returns and use **Cholesky Decomposition** to generate correlated random shocks.
- **Scale**: The default simulation runs 1,000 paths over a 21-day horizon, aggregating the portfolio returns at the end of the horizon to compute the simulated VaR.

## 2. Credit Risk Modeling

### Probability of Default (PD)
We treat the prediction of a borrower defaulting as a binary classification problem.
- **Features**: The model ingests key financial ratios:
  - Leverage Ratio (Debt / EBITDA)
  - Interest Coverage Ratio (EBITDA / Interest Expense)
  - Current Ratio (Current Assets / Current Liabilities)
  - Revenue Growth
- **Algorithm**: `LogisticRegression` (via scikit-learn). Chosen for its interpretability. The output is a probability score between 0.0 and 1.0.

### Expected Credit Loss (ECL)
A standard regulatory metric for credit risk.
- Formula: `ECL = PD * LGD * EAD`
- **PD** (Probability of Default): Generated by our ML model.
- **LGD** (Loss Given Default): Assigned based on collateral. Borrowers with collateral have a lower LGD (e.g., 40%) compared to unsecured borrowers (e.g., 75%).
- **EAD** (Exposure at Default): The total outstanding loan amount (drawn + undrawn commitments).

### Concentration Risk
We measure portfolio concentration using the **Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)**.
- Formula: The sum of the squares of the market shares (or portfolio weights) of the individual sectors.
- Interpretation: An HHI below 0.15 indicates a well-diversified portfolio, while an HHI above 0.25 indicates high concentration in a few specific sectors, triggering a risk alert.
